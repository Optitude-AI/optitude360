Why senior appointments fail

Credentials describe history. They do not guarantee judgement.

A candidate can look right on paper and still be wrong for the role, the board and the moment. Conventional search optimises for access — who is known, who is available, who is willing to move. Access matters, but it is not the same as fit.

The problem with the CV

Credentials demonstrate history. They do not automatically demonstrate judgement, adaptability, resilience, behaviour under pressure, organisational fit, or the ability to lead through ambiguity.

Our response

Executive Search Psychology brings the discipline of psychology to the judgement of leadership — assessing not only what an executive has done, but how they think, how they decide, and how they will behave under pressure.

How it works

A method that converts evidence into judgement.

  1. Brief

    We define the role in behavioural and psychological terms — not just responsibilities, but the temperament and judgement the position will require.

  2. Map

    We build a picture of the market and the candidate landscape, identifying where the required profile is most likely to be found.

  3. Assess

    We evaluate each candidate against the Optitude Core — Attitude, Aptitude and Fortitude — using structured, evidence-based methods.

  4. Decide

    We weigh candidates side by side, stress-test the shortlist, and support you in reaching a defensible, unhurried conclusion.

  5. Land

    We support a successful transition, anticipating where a strong appointment most often falters in its first year.
