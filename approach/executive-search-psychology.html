Executive Search Psychology

Optitude360 approaches executive search not as a matching exercise, but as an exercise in judgement. The right leadership appointment is rarely decided by a longer list of candidates or a faster shortlist; it is decided by how deeply an organisation understands the person it is bringing in, and how honestly it weighs the demands the role will actually place on them.

Psychology before process

Most search firms lead with process: mapping, sourcing, long-listing, interviewing. We do not. Process is essential, but it is the instrument, not the insight. Our starting point is a psychological definition of the role — what the position will demand of a leader's behaviour, temperament and judgement, not merely what it will demand of their CV. When the human requirements are understood first, every subsequent step of the search becomes sharper and faster, and the risk of a polished but wrong appointment falls away.

Judgement over CV

A CV tells you what someone has done; it tells you little about how they will do it. We treat the CV as evidence to be interrogated, not as a candidate. Our assessment is weighted towards the qualities that determine real performance — the ability to decide well under uncertainty, to read a room, to hold a line when it is costly, and to recover quickly from error. We are far less interested in whether a candidate has the right job titles than in whether they possess the right cast of mind.

Fit that lasts

Short-term fit is easy to manufacture and easy to mistake for the real thing. We focus on fit that survives the honeymoon: the alignment of a leader's natural wiring with the culture, pace and expectations of the organisation they will actually enter. This means understanding the environment as rigorously as we understand the individual. A search is only successful when the appointment still looks right eighteen months on — not just on the day the offer is signed.

Start with a conversation about judgement, not a search brief.
